Soidyn Studio XBand is a Mac app that makes sound feel 3D in your headphones. Try it below.

Download for Mac

What the app does

3D sound for headphones

It takes sound from up to 32 channels and turns it into 3D sound for your headphones. The sound comes in through a virtual input called Soidyn Driver.

9.1.4 plus objects

A base layout of 14 channels: nine around you, one for bass, and four above you. On top of that, you can place up to 18 sounds anywhere in 3D.

Move sounds live

You can move a sound while the audio is playing. Your DAW sends the positions over UDP or OSC. A Reaper script is included.

Render to file

Turn a surround soundtrack, like 5.1 or 7.1.4, into a file that sounds 3D in headphones.

How your brain finds a sound

Time

Sound from your right reaches your right ear a fraction of a millisecond before your left. Your brain reads that gap as direction.

Loudness

Your head blocks some of the sound, so the far ear hears it a little quieter, especially at high pitches.

Shape

Your ears, head, and shoulders change a sound before it reaches your eardrum. A model of this is called an HRTF. With it, a sound can seem to come from above, below, or behind you. The demo uses the one built into your browser.

From two channels to a whole space

Stereo

Two channels. Sound lives somewhere between the left and right speaker.

Surround

Formats like 5.1 mix sound into fixed speakers around you, in a flat ring.

Object-based

Each sound carries a position in 3D, height included. The system then renders it for your speakers or headphones.

Installation

  1. Install the driver. Unzip SoidynDriver-1.2.0.pkg.zip, open SoidynDriver-1.2.0.pkg, and follow the steps. If macOS blocks it, right-click the file, choose Open, and confirm. You do not need to restart your Mac. If Soidyn Driver does not show up, reopen Audio MIDI Setup.
  2. Install the app. Unzip the file and drag Soidyn Studio XBand.app into your Applications folder.
  3. Open the app. If your Mac blocks it the first time, right-click the app, choose Open, and confirm. Say yes when it asks for microphone access, because the app listens to Soidyn Driver.

Help and answers

To check that a download is complete and unchanged, open Terminal and run shasum -a 256 followed by the file name. The result should match the SHA-256 code above.

Soidyn Driver does not show up

Close Audio MIDI Setup and open it again. If it is still missing, the installer log is at /var/log/soidyn-install.log.

My Mac will not open the app

Right-click the app, choose Open, then confirm. You only need to do this the first time.

The app says “Virtual input (Soidyn Driver) not found”

The driver is not installed yet. Install Soidyn Driver first, then open the app again.

I hear no sound

While the app runs, it sets your Mac’s sound output to Soidyn Driver, and it puts the old output back when you stop. Make sure the app that plays the sound uses that output, and that your headphones are connected.

Why does the app ask for microphone access?

macOS uses that name for any audio input. The app only listens to Soidyn Driver. If you said no by mistake, turn it on in System Settings, under Privacy & Security, then Microphone.

Does it work on Apple silicon Macs?

The app is built for Intel Macs. It has not been tested on Apple silicon yet, so it may not run. The driver installer supports both.

Where can I find the app’s log?

The log is saved at ~/Library/Logs/SoidynStudioII.log. It helps a lot when you report a problem.

Your audio stays on your Mac

Soidyn Studio XBand processes all audio on your computer. It does not upload your audio. The app’s own scripts make no internet connections.

The only network ports it uses are local ones, on 127.0.0.1, so a DAW on the same Mac can send object positions.
